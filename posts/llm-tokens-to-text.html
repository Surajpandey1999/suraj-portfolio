Inside a Large Language Model: Tokens to Text

What actually happens between you pressing Enter and an LLM replying? Tokenization, embeddings, attention, next-token prediction — the full journey explained in plain language, no PhD required.

By Suraj Pandey•9 August 2026•11 min read

The Full Journey, At a Glance

This whole loop repeats once per word generated in the reply.

Step 1: Tokenization

The model can't read raw text. First, your prompt is broken into tokens — small chunks that can be whole words, parts of words, or punctuation.

UnderstandingLLMsisfun!

Notice "Understanding" split into 3 tokens and "LLMs" into 2 — uncommon words get broken into smaller familiar pieces.

Step 2: Embeddings

Each token is converted into a list of numbers (a vector) called an embedding, representing its meaning in a mathematical space. Words with similar meanings end up as similar numbers — "king" and "queen" are numerically closer to each other than "king" and "banana."

Step 3: Attention

Every token then looks at every other token in the prompt and decides how relevant each one is to understanding it in context — this is the self-attention mechanism (covered in depth in the Transformers article). This is how the model understands that "it" in a sentence refers back to a specific earlier noun.

Step 4: Next-Token Prediction

After processing through many attention layers, the model outputs a probability for every possible next token in its vocabulary — tens of thousands of options — and picks one (usually the most likely, sometimes with some randomness for variety).

Step 5: Repeat

That chosen token gets added to the sequence, and the entire process runs again to pick the next one. This is why LLMs generate text one token at a time — each response is actually thousands of these prediction cycles happening in sequence, fast enough to feel instant.

Putting It All Together

Prompt → tokens → embeddings → attention → probability over next token → pick one → repeat. There's no separate "understanding" step hidden somewhere — the model's apparent understanding emerges from this repeated prediction process at massive scale.

Conclusion

Once you see this loop clearly, LLMs stop feeling like magic — they're an extremely sophisticated pattern-completion engine, repeating a simple loop millions of times over, trained on enough data that the patterns it learned look a lot like understanding.
