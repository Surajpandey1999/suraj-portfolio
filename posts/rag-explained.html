RAG: Retrieval-Augmented Generation Explained

Why LLMs hallucinate, and how connecting them to your own data with RAG fixes it — with a real architecture diagram.

By Suraj Pandey•9 August 2026•7 min read

The Problem: LLMs Don't Know Your Data

An LLM only knows what it learned during training — it has no idea about your company's internal documents, yesterday's news, or anything created after its training cutoff. When asked, it often guesses confidently and gets it wrong. This is called hallucination.

The Fix: Retrieval-Augmented Generation

RAG solves this by retrieving relevant information from your own data before the model generates an answer, and feeding that information into the prompt as context — so the model answers from real, current facts instead of guessing.

The model's answer is grounded in your real documents — not just what it memorized during training.

How the "Search" Step Works

Your documents are broken into chunks and converted into embeddings — numeric representations of meaning — stored in a vector database. When a question comes in, it's also converted to an embedding, and the database finds the most semantically similar chunks, not just keyword matches.

Why RAG Matters

  • Answers stay grounded in real, current data
  • No need to retrain the model when your data changes
  • Works with private/internal documents the model never saw during training
  • Cheaper than fine-tuning for most use cases

Common Use Cases

  • Customer support chatbots answering from a company's documentation
  • Internal knowledge assistants searching company wikis
  • Legal/medical tools referencing specific case documents

Conclusion

RAG is one of the most practical techniques in applied GenAI — it turns a general-purpose LLM into a system that can answer accurately about your specific, current data without expensive retraining.
