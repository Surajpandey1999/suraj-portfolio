Transformers & the Attention Mechanism Explained

Self-attention, positional encoding, and why transformers replaced RNNs — the architecture behind every modern LLM, with a diagram.

By Suraj Pandey•9 August 2026•10 min read

The Problem With Older Models (RNNs)

Before transformers, models like RNNs read text one word at a time, in order — like reading a sentence with your finger, unable to jump ahead or look back easily. This made them slow to train and bad at connecting words that were far apart in a sentence.

The Big Idea: Self-Attention

Transformers read the entire sentence at once and let every word directly "look at" every other word, deciding how much attention to pay to each one. This is called self-attention.

Self-attention: every word looks at every other word and weighs how relevant it is.

Why This Matters

  • Processes all words in parallel — much faster to train than RNNs
  • Handles long-range relationships between words easily
  • Same core mechanism scales from small models to GPT-scale LLMs

Positional Encoding

Since transformers read all words at once (not in order), they lose track of word order. To fix this, a positional encoding — a small numeric signal representing each word's position — is added to each word before processing, so the model still knows sequence order.

Multi-Head Attention

Instead of computing attention once, transformers do it several times in parallel ("heads"), each learning to focus on a different kind of relationship — one head might track grammar, another might track subject-object relationships. The results are combined at the end.

Encoder vs Decoder

  • Encoder — reads and understands input (used in models like BERT)
  • Decoder — generates output one token at a time (used in GPT-style models like Claude)

Conclusion

Every major LLM today — GPT, Claude, Gemini, LLaMA — is built on this same transformer architecture. Understanding self-attention is the single most important concept for understanding how modern AI actually processes language.
