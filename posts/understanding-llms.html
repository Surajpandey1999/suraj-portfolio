Understanding Large Language Models

How LLMs like GPT and Claude are trained, what "parameters" actually means, and why scale changes behavior.

By Suraj Pandey•9 August 2026•9 min read

What is a Large Language Model?

An LLM is a neural network trained on massive amounts of text to predict the next word in a sequence. Do that well enough, at large enough scale, and the model develops the ability to write, reason, summarize, translate, and hold conversations.

What Are "Parameters"?

Parameters are the individual numbers (weights) inside the neural network that get adjusted during training — think of them as tiny dials the model learns to set correctly. GPT-3 had 175 billion of them; modern frontier models have even more. More parameters generally means more capacity to learn complex patterns.

More parameters = more capacity to capture complex patterns in language (roughly — not the only factor).

How Training Works (Simplified)

  1. Pre-training — the model reads a huge portion of the internet, books, and code, learning to predict the next word over and over, billions of times.
  2. Fine-tuning — the base model is further trained on curated examples to follow instructions well.
  3. RLHF (Reinforcement Learning from Human Feedback) — humans rank different responses, and the model is adjusted to prefer the responses people rate as better, more helpful, and safer.

Why Scale Changes Behavior

As models get bigger and see more training data, they don't just get "a little better" — certain abilities appear somewhat suddenly at scale, like multi-step reasoning or following complex instructions. This is often called an "emergent capability" — the model wasn't explicitly taught these skills, but they show up once scale and training data cross a certain threshold.

What LLMs Are Actually Good and Bad At

  • Good at: writing, summarizing, explaining, translating, pattern completion, code generation
  • Struggles with: precise arithmetic on large numbers, knowing things after its training cutoff, being 100% factually reliable without external tools (like search or RAG)

Conclusion

LLMs aren't magic — they're the result of a next-word-prediction objective applied at massive scale, refined through fine-tuning and human feedback. Understanding parameters, training stages, and scale effects gives you a solid mental model for how tools like GPT and Claude actually work under the hood.
